SIA 2 – Lesson 1: Advanced Enterprise Architecture Foundations
Prepared by: Instructor.
2nd Semester, AY 2025–2026.

Learning Outcomes
At the end of the lesson, students should be able to:
Explain the role of enterprise architecture in system integration.
Differentiate the EA domains.
Evaluate EA frameworks for a given organization.

Why Enterprise Architecture?
Organizations run dozens of systems that were built at different times by different teams.
Without a shared blueprint, systems overlap, data is duplicated and integration becomes expensive.
Enterprise architecture (EA) gives leaders a complete view of how business, data, applications and technology fit together.

Definition
Enterprise Architecture is the practice of analyzing, designing, planning and implementing enterprise analysis to successfully execute business strategies.
It bridges business goals and IT capabilities.
EA is not a one-time project; it is a continuous process.

EA Domains (BDAT)
Business Architecture – Defines strategy, governance, organization and key business processes.
Data Architecture – Describes the structure of logical and physical data assets and data management resources.
Application Architecture – Provides a blueprint for individual applications, their interactions and relationships to business processes.
Technology Architecture – Describes the software and hardware capabilities that support business, data and application services.

As-Is vs To-Be
Baseline (As-Is) Architecture: the current state of the enterprise.
Target (To-Be) Architecture: the desired future state.
Gap Analysis: identifies what must change to move from baseline to target.
Transition Architecture: an intermediate state between baseline and target.

Popular EA Frameworks
TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) – Most widely used; centered on the Architecture Development Method (ADM).
Zachman Framework – A 6x6 matrix that classifies artifacts by audience and by what, how, where, who, when and why.
FEAF – Used by the U.S. federal government to standardize IT across agencies.
Gartner EA Framework – Focuses on business outcomes rather than documentation.

TOGAF ADM Cycle
Preliminary.
A. Architecture Vision.
B. Business Architecture.
C. Information Systems Architectures.
D. Technology Architecture.
E. Opportunities & Solutions.
F. Migration Planning.
G. Implementation Governance.
H. Architecture Change Management.
Requirements Management sits at the center of the cycle.

Key Concepts
Stakeholder – any person or group with an interest in the system or its architecture.
Concern – an interest a stakeholder has in the architecture.
View – a representation of a system from the perspective of related concerns.
Viewpoint – the conventions for creating a view.
Artifact – an architectural work product such as a catalog, matrix or diagram.
Architecture Repository – stores all architecture outputs of the enterprise.

Benefits of EA
Better alignment of IT investments with business goals.
Reduced complexity and redundancy of systems.
Faster and better decision-making.
Easier integration of new systems.
Lower operating costs.

Common Pitfalls
Treating EA as documentation only – diagrams nobody reads.
Lack of executive sponsorship leads to failed EA programs.
Ivory-tower architecture: plans that ignore how people actually work.
Trying to model everything at once causes the effort to stall.

EA and System Integration
EA identifies which systems must exchange data and how.
Integration patterns such as point-to-point, hub-and-spoke and enterprise service bus are chosen based on the target architecture.
Point-to-point integration is simpler than an ESB for a few systems, but an ESB is more scalable than point-to-point for many systems.

Case: State University
The university runs separate enrollment, library, accounting and HR systems.
Student records are entered three times in different systems.
The EA team performed a gap analysis and proposed a single student master data source.
Result: fewer data errors and faster enrollment.

Summary
EA aligns business and IT through the BDAT domains.
Frameworks like TOGAF and Zachman guide the work.
Gap analysis connects the current and target states.

Activity
In groups, draw the As-Is architecture of your school's enrollment process.
Identify at least three gaps.
