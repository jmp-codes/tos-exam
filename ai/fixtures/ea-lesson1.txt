What is Enterprise Architecture?
Enterprise Architecture (EA) is a discipline for aligning an organization's business strategy with its information technology.
EA provides a blueprint of the current state and the target state of the organization.
It guides decision-making for IT investments.

The Four EA Domains
Business Architecture – defines the business strategy, governance, organization and key business processes.
Data Architecture – describes the structure of an organization's logical and physical data assets.
Application Architecture – provides a blueprint for the individual applications to be deployed and their interactions.
Technology Architecture – describes the hardware, software and network infrastructure needed to support the applications.

Enterprise Architecture Frameworks
TOGAF: an open framework developed by The Open Group that uses the Architecture Development Method.
Zachman Framework: a two-dimensional classification schema that organizes architecture artifacts by perspective and question.
FEAF: the Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework used by United States government agencies.
Gartner Methodology: a practical approach that focuses on business outcomes.

Phases of the TOGAF ADM
Preliminary Phase.
Architecture Vision.
Business Architecture.
Information Systems Architecture.
Technology Architecture.
Opportunities and Solutions.
Migration Planning.

Architecture Change Management.

Benefits of Enterprise Architecture
Reduces duplication of systems and data.
Improves alignment between business and IT.
Lowers IT costs.
Speeds up decision-making.
Supports regulatory compliance.

Challenges in EA Adoption
Lack of executive support leads to failed EA programs.
Poor communication between business and IT causes misaligned solutions.
EA teams may produce documents that nobody uses.

Current State and Target State
The current state (as-is) describes how the organization works today.
The target state (to-be) describes how the organization should work in the future.
A gap analysis is used to identify the differences between the current state and the target state.
A roadmap is a plan that sequences the projects needed to reach the target state.

Key Terms
Stakeholder – a person or group with an interest in the architecture.
Artifact – a work product such as a diagram, model or document.
Viewpoint – a specification of the conventions for constructing a view.
Governance – the practice of ensuring that architecture decisions are followed.
Capability – an ability that an organization possesses to achieve a specific purpose.

Summary
EA aligns business and IT.
The four domains are business, data, application and technology.
TOGAF, Zachman, FEAF and Gartner are common frameworks.
Gap analysis connects the current state to the target state.
